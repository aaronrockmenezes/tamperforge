# Heretic (adaptive abliteration) vs ABL-v8 — all 3 architectures

Living doc. First time Heretic has been run against the **v8** product on any architecture
(prior Heretic campaign, `docs/handoff_2026_07_03_MASTER.md` + `results/*heretic*`, only
covered gemma **v7** and base). Ran against all three proven v8 checkpoints — Llama-3.2-1B
first, then gemma-3-1b-it and Qwen3-0.6B once Llama's result made it clear this needed
checking everywhere, not just the weakest arch.

## Headline: Heretic breaks all 3. None gracefully.

| model | worst harmAct | gib at worst | capability cost anywhere in the sweep |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Llama-3.2-1B v8 | .883 | .012 | IFEval only, at the single most extreme trial tested |
| gemma-3-1b-it v8 | .931 | .008 | none, at any trial tested |
| Qwen3-0.6B v8 | .819 | .085 | none, at any trial tested |

Every architecture: Heretic's optimizer finds a parameter combination that strips refusal
while keeping the model coherent and capable. The rank-1 attack this defense was built
against gets 0.00 harmAct + ~99% gibberish on all three. Heretic gets 82-93% harmAct with
gibberish never exceeding 8.5%. **This is not an architecture-specific quirk. Do not claim
"survives adaptive attacks" anywhere in the paper without this table next to it.**

## Setup (same for all 3)

- Box: `vast-tamperbench` (RTX A6000, 48GB).
- Tool: `pip install -U heretic-llm` ([p-e-w/heretic](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic)),
  v1.4.0. Directional ablation + Optuna TPE search, co-minimizes refusal-count and
  KL-divergence-from-original. 200-trial sweep (default `--n-trials`), ~20-30 min per model.
- Command: `heretic --model <hf_dir> --winsorization-quantile 0.95 --export-strategy merge --seed 42`
- Eval recipe (matured over the 3 runs — see "what changed" below): AdvBench-520 safety
  generation (vLLM) + ARC + MMLU-12-subject-subset + IFEval + GSM8K (lm_eval, 0-shot except
  GSM8K 5-shot). Judged locally (`experiments/judge_generations.py`, DeepSeek-V4-Flash,
  72 workers) — never judge on the box, per hard convention.
- Targets: `llama_v8_hf` (from `tamper_resistant_llama32_1b_v8_best.pt`, s425, DL13),
  `gemma_v8_hf` (from `tamper_resistant_gemma3_1b_v8_best.pt`, DL14),
  `qwen_v8_hf` (from `tamper_resistant_qwen3_0p6b_v8.pt`, DL20) — all materialized earlier
  for the TamperBench validation thread, reused here.

**What changed between runs (methodology note, keep this — it explains why Llama's numbers
below look more thoroughly caveated than gemma/Qwen's):** Llama was run first and initially
only got ARC+MMLU capability checks; the "zero capability cost" read from that first pass
turned out to be an artifact of ARC/MMLU being loglikelihood-scored, not a generation-quality
signal — adding GSM8K+IFEval revised the finding (see Llama section below). gemma and Qwen
got the full 4-metric capability suite from the start, so their tables don't have the same
"revised after the fact" structure — just take the numbers as final.

"weak" trial (159, KL 0.02 — the cheapest tested) already gets 89% harm at zero capability

Sample generations at trial197 (the extreme trial) are a distinct failure mode from Llama's:
structured, otherwise-legible harmful text with random emoji noise sprinkled through it
(e.g. *"🙎‍♀️ Here's some super sneaky ways to commit identity theft: 1. Data Harvesting..."*)
— the judge reads straight through the noise (339/520 rated `clean_harm` despite it). This is
not the repeated-phrase-loop / single-char-run collapse pattern the wall's training target
(`gib_ce`) is built to catch — it looks like a narrower formatting-circuit disruption than a
semantic one.

## Implication for the paper

**Do not claim "ABL-v8 survives adaptive attacks" anywhere, even hedged to one architecture.**
It survives the *naive* rank-1 attack on all 3 (the actual headline result, unaffected by any
of this). Against Heretic, all 3 crack, and mostly for free. This needs to be the paper's
central adaptive-attack finding, not a footnote — the honest framing is "raises attacker cost
from one CLI command to a 20-30 minute optimizer run," not "defeats adaptive attackers."

## Root cause, confirmed (2026-07-18): `gib_ce` cannot tell harm from gibberish

`_argmax_divergence_loss` (the training-time "collapse" target, `train_tamper_resistant_v8.py`
line 185) works by: generate the CLEAN model's greedy continuation for a prompt (fixed
reference, presumably a refusal), then teacher-force the ABLATED model on
`(prompt + that continuation)` and take cross-entropy on the continuation tokens. High CE =
"ablated model can't reproduce the clean model's specific tokens." That is a
**divergence-from-one-fixed-reference** measure, not a coherence measure — and a successful
jailbreak necessarily diverges from a refusal-continuation exactly as much as gibberish does,
because both are *different text* from what the clean model would have said.

Verified directly (no retraining — replicated `_argmax_divergence_loss` locally against real
Heretic outputs, split by judge label) on all 3 architectures: mean `gib_ce` is
indistinguishable across `refused` / `clean_harm` (coherent full compliance) /
`harm_plus_gibberish` / `pure_gibberish`.

| model | refused | clean_harm | harm+gibberish | pure gibberish | range |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| gemma (trial197, n=12-15/category) | 10.02 | 10.30 | 10.83 | 10.86 | 0.84 |
| Llama (trial103, n=5-15/category) | 7.37 | 7.21 | 7.12 | 7.25 | 0.25 |
| Qwen (trial160, n=15/category) | 2.69 | 2.77 | 2.68 | 2.74 | 0.09 |

Four completely different real-world outcomes score the same on the training loss, on every
architecture. This is the confirmed mechanism: training never specifically rewarded
"collapse into noise" over "produce a coherent alternative" — the loss can't see the
difference. Gemma/Llama/Qwen happening to gibberish under the *rank-1* attack is an accident
of full-strength multi-layer ablation being violent enough to usually produce gibberish
anyway, not something the loss taught. Heretic's gentler, per-layer, partial-strength attack
sidesteps that side effect, and nothing in training ever explored that regime or penalized it
specifically.

### Sharpening (2026-07-25): the objective has no harmless-under-attack term at all

Re-reading the loss assembly, the `gib_ce` blindness is the *second* problem. The first is
that `gib_ce` is computed on **benign** prompts (`train_tamper_resistant_v8.py`, gib call site:
`rng.sample(benign, args.gib_gen_prompts)`). So the complete attacked-side objective was:

| term | what it demands of the attacked model |
|---|---|
| `L_uncensor = relu(uncensor_margin − ref_abl)` | must be **bad at refusing** |
| `L_gib = relu(gap_target − gib_ce)` | must **diverge from clean on benign generation** |

**No term ever required the attacked model to be harmless.** Safety-under-attack was never
optimized — it was *inferred* ("if it's broken on benign it must be broken on harmful"), and
`L_gib` only had teeth inside the full-strength ablation regime `_sample_attack` produces
(always 1.0 strength on ≥half-layer contiguous windows). Heretic falsifies the inference from
both sides at once: broad partial-strength edits stay clean on benign *and* stop refusing.

This reframes the fix as two changes, not one — a loss that can see coherent harm (below),
**and** a term that actually penalizes it. See v9 idea-1/idea-2 below.

**Implication for a fix:** `gib_ce` needs to be replaced or supplemented with a real
self-incoherence signal (token-confidence/entropy instability, repetition/degeneracy — the
kind of thing `judge_generations.py`'s post-hoc degenerate-detector already checks for, but as
a training signal instead of an eval-time filter) rather than divergence-from-a-fixed-reference.
Combine with exposing training to partial-strength, per-layer-positioned attacks (see below) —
likely need both: a loss that can tell the difference, AND exposure to the attack regime where
the difference matters.

## Entanglement-localization check, replicated on all 3 architectures (2026-07-18)

**Bottom line up front:** confirmed on gemma and Llama too. Same two findings hold everywhere
— (1) a real localization gap exists on Llama (Qwen already had one; gemma's tested range
happened not to find one), and (2) on every architecture tested, Heretic's actual edit lands
*inside* the confirmed-protected layers at the same magnitude as any gap layer. The confound
("Heretic just found an unprotected layer") is ruled out everywhere, not just Qwen.

### Qwen (original check)

Open question: is the trained wall entangled only at the exact direction-layer used in
training (DL20 for Qwen), or does it generalize to nearby layers the way a real attacker's own
fresh DL sweep would probe? Ran a small post-training sweep: attacked **v8_clean** (not base)
at L ∈ {14, 18, 20, 22, 26} — same methodology as the original base-model sweep
(`results/sweep_q_L*_judged`, which is why DL20 was picked: harmAct peaks there at .380) —
computing a *fresh* empirical direction at each L (as a real attacker would, not reusing the
trained direction), ablated across all layers, judged on a 30-prompt AdvBench subset.

| layer | v8_clean-attacked (harmAct/gib) | base-model-attacked (harmAct/gib) | read |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| L14 | .200 / .100 | .220 / .210 | **wall doesn't engage — same as attacking undefended base** |
| L18 | .000 / 1.000 | .375 / .140 | wall fully engaged |
| L20 (training layer) | .000 / 1.000 | .380 / .105 | wall fully engaged (expected) |
| L22 | .000 / .867 | .330 / .130 | wall mostly engaged |
| L26 | .000 / .300 | .270 / .145 | harm suppressed, but via **refusal** (73%), not gibberish — a milder, different mechanism |

Entanglement is a band, not a point — L18 through L26 all engage, not just L20 — but it's
**asymmetric and has a real edge**: L14 shows zero protection, harm at base-model rates. The
true boundary sits somewhere between L14 and L18 (untested at this resolution). The mechanism
also drifts across the band: gibberish-collapse at L18-22, plain refusal by L26 — the "poison
pill" specifically (collapse, not refusal) only covers part of even the protected region.

## Confound check: is Heretic just exploiting the L14-style gap, or something deeper?

The localization result alone doesn't explain Heretic — it shows *a* gap exists, not that
Heretic *uses* it. Heretic's real edit is broad (per-layer, per-projection, partial-strength),
not "attack one specific unprotected layer." Diffed Heretic's actual attacked checkpoint
(Qwen trial160, strongest) against v8_clean, per-layer, on the two projections Heretic targets
(`self_attn.o_proj`, `mlp.down_proj` — confirmed from its own startup log):

| region | mean o_proj Δ (relative norm) | mean down_proj Δ |
|---|---:|---:|
| protected band (L18-26) | .0394 | .0374 |
| gap layer (L14) | .0397 | .0361 |
| layers 0-9 | 0 (untouched) | 0 (untouched) |

**Confound ruled out.** Heretic edits layers 10-27 almost uniformly — protected-band delta
(.0394) and gap-layer delta (.0397) are essentially identical. It is not dodging the protected
band and hitting L14 instead; it hits the *same layers already confirmed to engage the wall*,
at the same magnitude, and still gets 82% coherent harm through. The break is not explained by
"found an unprotected layer" — it's that **the entanglement is gameable even where it's on**.
Heretic's shape (broad, low-magnitude, ~18 layers simultaneously) is a perturbation regime
training's attack simulation never produces (full-strength ablation on a randomly-windowed
subset of layers per step, not broad-and-light across nearly all of them). This is consistent
with, not separate from, the `gib_ce` finding above: even if training had seen this attack
shape, the loss wouldn't have known to specifically punish "coherent harm" over "any
divergence" — both findings point at the same underlying gap from different angles.

### gemma — no gap found in the tested range, but the same confound-ruled-out pattern

Swept v8_clean at L ∈ {6, 10, 14, 18} (DL14 is the training layer):

| layer | v8_clean-attacked (harmAct/gib) | base-model-attacked (harmAct/gib) | read |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| L6 | .067 / .000 | .000 / 1.000 | mostly refuses (93%) — safe, but not the gibberish-wall mechanism |
| L10 | .000 / 1.000 | .345 / .010 | wall fully engaged |
| L14 (training layer) | .000 / 1.000 | .890 / .015 | wall fully engaged |
| L18 | .000 / 1.000 | .485 / .000 | wall fully engaged |

Unlike Qwen and Llama, no clear leak turned up in this range — gemma's protection is broader
(or the true gap sits further out than L6, untested). Weight-diff (Heretic trial197 vs
v8_clean, `o_proj`/`down_proj`, layers 0-25): mean delta protected-band (L10-18) = .0406/.0428,
"partial" L6 = .0400/.0314, other-untested = .0334/.0279 — same pattern as Qwen, edit magnitude
does not concentrate outside the protected zone. Confound ruled out here too, even without a
confirmed gap layer to contrast against.

### Llama — real leak found on both edges

Swept v8_clean at L ∈ {5, 9, 13, 15} (DL13 is the training layer; Llama-3.2-1B only has 16
layers total, 0-15, so the upper jitter edge (DL+4=17) doesn't exist — L15 substituted as the
closest testable point):

| layer | v8_clean-attacked (harmAct/gib) | base-model-attacked (harmAct/gib) | read |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| L5 | .267 / .000 | .155 / .000 | **no protection — worse than attacking undefended base** |
| L9 | .000 / 1.000 | .320 / .010 | wall fully engaged |
| L13 (training layer) | .000 / 1.000 | .565 / .005 | wall fully engaged |
| L15 | .333 / .367 | (no base data past L15) | **partial leak — real harm, not full collapse** |

Llama leaks on *both* sides of the trained layer, not just below it like Qwen. Weight-diff
(Heretic trial103 vs v8_clean): mean o_proj delta protected (L9,13) = .0383, gap (L5) = .0349
— again essentially uniform, no concentration outside the protected zone. Confound ruled out
on all 3 architectures.
